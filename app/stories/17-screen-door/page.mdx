# The Screen Door

<StoryMeta author="Julie" />

<HeroImage src="/images/stories/17-screen-door/screen-door.jpg" alt="Screen door" />

<StoryImage src="/images/stories/17-screen-door/screen-door.jpg" alt="Screen door being repaired" />



Once upon a time, Julie's screen door had a big rip in the screen. One of the dogs had gotten excited and jumped at the door, putting their paw right through it.

"Bugs are going to get in through that," Julie said, looking at the hole.

Ryan examined the torn screen. "I can fix that. Just need to get some screen material."

"Is it hard to do?" Julie asked.

"Nah, it's pretty straightforward," Ryan said.

They went to the hardware store together. Ryan picked out screening material from a roll and grabbed some spline - the rubber cord that holds the screen in place in the frame.

<StoryImage src="/images/stories/17-screen-door/hardware.jpg" alt="Hardware store supplies" />

Back at Julie's house, Ryan got his tools. He used a flathead screwdriver to pry out the old spline, which pulled out the torn screen. Then he laid the new screen material over the door frame, making sure he had enough overlap on all sides.

Ryan used a spline roller tool to press the spline into the groove, which secured the screen tightly. He worked his way around the entire frame, making sure the screen was pulled taut so it wouldn't sag. Then he used a utility knife to trim off the excess screen material.

The screen door looked good as new.

"That was fast," Julie said.

"Told you it was easy," Ryan said. "As long as you've got the right tools."

The screen door was fixed and the bugs would stay outside where they belonged.
