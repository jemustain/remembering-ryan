# Girlfriend at Work

<StoryMeta author="Julie" />

<HeroImage src="/images/stories/08-girlfriend-at-work/hero.jpg" alt="" />

Ryan had a packed day. He had several landscaping stops lined up, a tree that needed cutting, a big yard cleanup, and a quote to give another customer. He needed help.

Ryan called around to see who could work. Only his employee was available that day. Two guys for all those stops wasn't really enough. So Ryan called Julie.

"Hey, want to come to work with me today? I could use an extra set of hands."

Julie had never done landscaping work before, but she said sure. Why not?

Ryan picked her up in his truck and they headed to the first job. He had to cut down part of a tree at someone's property. Ryan did most of the work with the chainsaw. He knew what he was doing and didn't want Julie anywhere near that thing. Once the tree was down, Julie and his employee helped clean up the property. They hauled branches, raked up debris, and got the yard looking right again.

The second stop was a big cleanup at another customer's house. This one was more Julie's speed. Ryan put her on the leaf blower. She walked around blowing leaves while Ryan trimmed the hedges and his guy cleaned up the grass clippings. Julie had never used a leaf blower before, but she got the hang of it pretty quick.

Then Ryan had to drive to another house to give a quote for a new customer. Julie sat in the truck while he walked around the property with the homeowner, talking through what needed to be done.

It was a full day. Several stops, a lot of driving, and a lot of work. Julie's arms were tired and she had dirt in places she didn't know dirt could get.

"Thanks for coming out today," Ryan said on the drive home. "Seriously, that helped a lot."

"It was actually kind of fun," Julie said.

She meant it. It wasn't her world, but she liked being part of his for a day. Seeing how he worked, how he talked to customers, how he kept everything moving. Ryan had a lot going on that day and he just handled it, one stop at a time.
