# Tortoises

<StoryMeta author="Julie" />

<HeroImage src="/images/stories/11-tortoises/hero.jpg" alt="" />

When Ryan moved in, he brought Balto and his four pet tortoises. He'd owned them for years — desert tortoises he'd gotten when they were small.

The tortoises had been living in a basic setup at Ryan's old place, but now that he was at Julie's house, Ryan wanted to build them something better.

"I'm going to build the tortoises a proper enclosure," Ryan said.

"How big?" Julie asked.

"Big enough that they've got room to move around. Maybe four feet by six feet."

Ryan went to the garage and started planning. He bought wood, sheets of plexiglass for the walls, hinges for the doors, and all the hardware he'd need. He had his tools already.

Ryan worked on the tortoise enclosure for several weeks. He'd come home from his construction jobs and head straight to the garage. He measured and cut each piece of wood carefully. He sanded all the edges smooth so there wouldn't be any rough spots that could hurt the tortoises. He built a wooden frame, then attached the plexiglass walls so you could see the tortoises from outside.

Some nights Ryan would be out in the garage until late, putting pieces together and making sure everything was level and secure. Julie would bring him dinner while he worked.

"You don't have to make it perfect," Julie said. "They're tortoises."

"I want it done right," Ryan said.

When the enclosure was finally done, it was solid and roomy. It had clear plexiglass walls so you could watch the tortoises, a dirt floor for them to walk on, rocks for them to climb over, a water dish, and hiding spots. Ryan had even built hinged doors on top so he could easily reach in to feed them or clean.

Ryan moved the four tortoises into their new home. They slowly walked around, exploring their new space with their steady, deliberate tortoise pace.

"That looks really good," Julie said.

"Yeah, it turned out alright," Ryan said.

Ryan had spent weeks building something that would keep his tortoises safe and comfortable for years to come. That's just how he approached things - if he was going to do it, he was going to do it right.
