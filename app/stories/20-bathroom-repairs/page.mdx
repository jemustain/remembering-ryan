# Bathroom Repairs

<StoryMeta author="Julie" />

<HeroImage src="/images/stories/20-bathroom-repairs/bathroom-repair.jpg" alt="Bathroom repairs" />

<StoryImage src="/images/stories/20-bathroom-repairs/bathroom-repair.jpg" alt="Bathroom being repaired" />



That leaky valve Ryan fixed turned out to be just the beginning. The leak had been going on longer than they realized, and water had been slowly damaging the bathroom.

A week after Ryan fixed the valve, Julie noticed the bathroom wall felt soft when she touched it. The drywall had absorbed water and was damaged. Then she noticed the shower tiles were loose. And the toilet was wobbling when you sat on it - the floor underneath had water damage too.

"Ryan, I think the bathroom has more problems," Julie said.

Ryan came and looked at everything. He pressed on the wall and his finger went right through the drywall. He checked the shower and found that water had gotten behind the tiles. The toilet flange was damaged from the water.

"This is a bigger job," Ryan said. "I need to fix the walls, the shower, and reset the toilet properly."

"How long will that take?" Julie asked.

"A few days, probably," Ryan said. "Maybe longer."

Ryan started the repairs that weekend. First, he cut out all the damaged drywall and let everything dry out completely. He bought new drywall sheets and cut them to size, screwed them into the studs, and taped and mudded all the seams. That took a couple days because the mud had to dry between coats.

Next, he fixed the shower. He removed the loose tiles, fixed the waterproofing underneath, and reset the tiles with fresh grout.

<StoryImage src="/images/stories/20-bathroom-repairs/tile-work.jpg" alt="Tile work in progress" />

Then he dealt with the toilet. He removed it completely, replaced the damaged flange and the wax ring, and reset the toilet on a solid floor.

The whole job took Ryan about a week of evenings after work. It wasn't a quick fix, but when he was done, the bathroom was completely solid again. No more soft walls, no more loose tiles, no more wobbly toilet.

"Looks good as new," Julie said.

"Better than new," Ryan said. "Everything's done right this time."

Ryan had turned a water-damaged disaster back into a functional bathroom. It took time and effort, but that's what big repairs required.
